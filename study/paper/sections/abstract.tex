\begin{abstract}
Weight-space programming (WSP) uses a language model to inspect and modify another model's parameters. We review early evidence and test repair, iteration, and preservation in one $\policy$ stacking task. Across 1,056 simulated episodes, five searches produced four nonzero edits and eleven identity proposals; none of the edits passed the frozen gate. A separate fixed-batch16 follow-up validated all 80 requested activation items but yielded no accepted repair. Held-out unchanged-policy comparisons disagreed on 37/64 and 29/64 outcomes. All four edits changed fixed-input outputs without establishing closed-loop improvement. Replay failures, incorrect diagnostic annotations, a restricted interface, and a preservation gate below observed repeat variability limit the negative conclusion. We argue for evaluating policy family and failure mechanism separately: localized control defects may be more amenable to repair than perceptual ambiguity or missing competence, but this remains an untested comparative hypothesis here. Targeted demonstrations establish promise, not routine, reliable policy improvement. Physical AI adds action-dependent observations, contact dynamics, and expensive behavioral validation. Our results motivate calibrated controls and matched reproductions, rather than a general verdict on WSP.
\end{abstract}
