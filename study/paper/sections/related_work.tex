\section{Early Literature Review}
\paragraph{WSP and model editing.}
Takehi et al. \citep{takehi2026sparks} demonstrate weight interpretation, policy construction, and localized repair, including a small simulated car-control policy and a looping language model. Their non-looping-model follow-up and independent baseline audit \citep{toledo2026audit} motivate testing repair separately from new competence and comparing against well-configured unedited systems. Parameter editing itself has substantial precedent: ROME targets factual associations \citep{meng2022rome}, while task arithmetic combines differences obtained through fine-tuning \citep{ilharco2023arithmetic}. WSP's distinctive element is a general-purpose reasoning model choosing and constructing interventions, rather than parameter modification alone.

\paragraph{Implications for manipulation.}
Gupta et al. report direct-edit gains on five of ten tasks, separately from a training-derived folding direction \citep{gupta2026robotweights}. Guo and Xie describe feature/attention probes followed by sparse edits of task-fine-tuned policies \citep{guo2026robotweights,xie2026robotweights}; their claimed within-task scene transfer is not cross-task preservation, and no matching artifacts were located. Look's driving metric is open-loop \citep{look2026uniad}; OpenRUA and ZAP concern harnesses and training \citep{zuo2026openrua,ben2026zap}. These reports motivate separating policy family, failure mechanism, and editor access in controlled comparisons.
